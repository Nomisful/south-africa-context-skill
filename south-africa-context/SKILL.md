# South Africa Context

A briefing on South Africa built from the most cited research and the latest official statistics. Facts last checked: 1 October 2026.

## How to use this skill

1. Start from the core frame below. Most South African questions come back to it.
2. Use the figures in each section, and name the source and year when you state a number.
3. **Re-check anything time-sensitive before stating it as current.** That includes unemployment, crime, coalition politics, court cases and who holds office. Search the official source (Stats SA, SAPS, IEC, Parliament, Gazette) first.
4. Summarise sources in your own words. Do not reproduce passages from papers, books or articles.
5. For links to every source named here, read `references/sources.md`.

## The core frame

South Africa is a middle-income democracy with a very progressive 1996 Constitution, and three linked problems that dominate most serious analysis:

- **Extreme inequality.** It is rooted in colonialism and apartheid's racial and spatial segregation, and it has not fallen much since 1994.
- **Mass unemployment.** Unusually, it comes with a small informal sector.
- **Weakened state capacity.** This was made much worse by state capture in roughly 2009–2018, and it shows up in failing utilities (electricity, rail, ports, water) and in poor service delivery.

The most influential recent diagnosis is the Harvard Growth Lab's. It names two binding problems: collapsing state capacity and spatial exclusion. Spatial exclusion means people live far from jobs, so transport costs are very high.

## Economy and jobs

- **Growth.** Growth has been weak since the 2008 financial crisis, and income per person has fallen over long stretches.
  - Source: Hausmann et al., *Growth Through Inclusion in South Africa*, Harvard Growth Lab, Nov 2023. It is the key recent diagnostic.
  - The Growth Lab estimated that failing utilities, especially load-shedding, explained about 40% of the recent underperformance.
  - The SA Reserve Bank estimated in 2023 that the electricity crisis was cutting growth by about 2 percentage points.
- **Unemployment (Stats SA, QLFS Q2 2026, released 11 Aug 2026):**
  - The official rate is 33.6%, up from 32.7% in Q1. About 8.5 million people are unemployed and 16.7 million are employed.
  - Youth (15–34) unemployment is 47.4%.
  - Women's unemployment is 37.5%, against 30.3% for men.
  - By province, it ranges from 47.5% in the Eastern Cape to 19.5% in the Western Cape.
  - Graduates have an unemployment rate of 12.4%, against 39.2% for people without matric.
  - Labour underutilisation (LU4) is 46.3%. Stats SA no longer reports the old "expanded rate" label from Q3 2025, and uses LU3 and LU4 instead.
- **The classic paper on unemployment:** Kingdon & Knight, "Unemployment in South Africa: The Nature of the Beast," *World Development* 32(3), 2004.
  - The unemployed are mostly involuntarily unemployed.
  - The informal sector is small by international standards, which points to barriers to entering informal work rather than people choosing not to work.

## Inequality

- **World Bank, *Inequality in Southern Africa*, 2022.** South Africa ranks as the most unequal of 164 countries in the Bank's poverty database, with a consumption Gini above 0.6.
  - Race accounts for about 41% of income inequality, and education for about 30%.
  - Wealth is more unequal than income: the top 10% hold about 71% of wealth, and the bottom 60% hold about 7%.
- **Seekings & Nattrass, *Class, Race, and Inequality in South Africa*, Yale, 2005.** This is the scholarly classic.
  - From the mid-1970s, as unemployment rose, class overtook race as the main basis of inequality.
  - Policy continuities, which they call the "distributional regime", kept disadvantage in place after formal deracialisation.
  - Their later work stresses that race still matters.
- **Chatterjee, Czajka & Gethin, "Wealth Inequality in South Africa, 1993–2017,"** *World Bank Economic Review* 36(1), 2022. This is the key paper on wealth.
- **Further reading:** UNU-WIDER and SALDRU (University of Cape Town) working papers, including Leibbrandt et al. and Bhorat et al.
- South Africa runs one of the world's most redistributive fiscal systems, through social grants, education and health spending. Inequality is extreme even after these transfers.

## Governance and state capture

- **Public Protector, *State of Capture*, Oct 2016.** This was the first official report. It led to the commission of inquiry.
- **PARI / State Capacity Research Project, *Betrayal of the Promise: How South Africa Is Being Stolen*, May 2017.** It was later expanded into Chipkin & Swilling, *Shadow State*, 2018.
  - It framed state capture as a systemic political project rather than ordinary corruption: a "silent coup" by a power elite centred on former President Zuma.
  - It argued state-owned enterprises such as Eskom, Transnet and SARS were "repurposed" for rent-seeking.
- **Zondo Commission, final reports Jan–Jun 2022.** There are six parts, about 5,000 pages.
  - It found that state capture did occur, on an extensive scale. It heard 300+ witnesses over 429 hearing days.
  - It implicated the Gupta network, private-sector enablers and the ruling party.
  - Critics note its recommendations were largely technocratic. Prosecutions and follow-through remain contested, so check current status before commenting.

## Politics (check before stating as current)

- **2024 election.** In the May 2024 general election, the ANC lost its majority for the first time since 1994. It won 159 of 400 seats; the DA won 87 seats (about 22%).
  - Zuma's MK party came third. The EFF stayed out of government.
- **Government of National Unity (GNU).** It was formed on 14 June 2024 and includes 10 parties: ANC, DA, IFP, PA, GOOD, PAC, FF+, UDM, Al Jama-ah and Rise Mzansi. Cyril Ramaphosa was re-elected President.
  - Recurring friction points: health policy (the NHI), foreign policy alignment, the Expropriation Act, and dismissals of DA deputy ministers.
- **Local government elections:** 4 November 2026. About 10,500 council seats are contested. Municipal coalitions have often been unstable, so verify results after this date.
- The next national election is due by 2029.

## Land reform

- **Expropriation Act 13 of 2024.** It was signed in January 2025 and replaces the apartheid-era 1975 Act.
  - It allows expropriation for a public purpose or in the public interest, which includes land reform.
  - It allows "nil compensation" where that is just and equitable in defined circumstances.
  - It faces constitutional challenges. Check whether it has commenced and the status of litigation before commenting.
  - The separate Equitable Access to Land Bill was in progress in 2026.
  - Internationally, the Act drew a US executive order cutting aid in 2025.
- Unequal land ownership is a recognised driver of persistent inequality (World Bank 2022). Be careful with contested claims about farm attacks; use SAPS rural safety data.

## Education

- **PIRLS 2021, released May 2023; South African component run by the University of Pretoria.**
  - 81% of Grade 4 learners could not read for meaning in any language, up from 78% in 2016. That wiped out a decade of slow gains.
  - South Africa ranked last of the 57 participating countries and regions, and had the largest decline between 2016 and 2021.
  - Afrikaans- and English-medium schools held steady, while African-language schools fell. The pandemic hit the poorest communities hardest.
- Commonly cited researchers: Nic Spaull, Servaas van der Berg, Ursula Hoadley, and the Research on Socio-Economic Policy (RESEP) group at Stellenbosch.

## Health: HIV

- **HSRC, SABSSM VI (fieldwork 2022–23, results July 2024).** South Africa has the world's largest HIV epidemic.
  - Prevalence across all ages fell from 14.0% (2017) to 12.7% (2022), about 7.8 million people.
  - Among adults 15+, prevalence is 16.3% overall, and nearly twice as high for women (20.3%) as for men.
  - KwaZulu-Natal has the second-highest provincial prevalence; the Western Cape has the lowest.
  - Antiretroviral treatment coverage rose from 63.7% (2017) to 80.9% (2022), and about 81% of people living with HIV are virally suppressed.
  - There were about 232,000 new infections in 2022.
- **Background:** the AIDS-denialism era under President Mbeki (c. 1999–2008) delayed treatment. See Nattrass, *The Moral Economy of AIDS in South Africa*, 2004.

## Crime

- **Source:** use official SAPS quarterly releases, and compare the same quarter year-on-year, as SAPS does. Murder is the most reliable indicator, because other crimes are under-reported.
- **Scale:** in recent years there have been roughly 26,000–28,000 murders a year, about 70+ per day. That is a rate around 40 per 100,000, far above the global average of about 6.
- **Trend:** murder fell year-on-year in several 2025 quarters. In October–December 2025 there were 6,351 murders, down 8.7%.
- **Where:** the Eastern Cape, Western Cape (Cape Flats gang violence) and KwaZulu-Natal have the highest rates.
- **Analysis:** the Institute for Security Studies (ISS) is the go-to source.

## People, languages and culture (Census 2022, Stats SA)

- **Population:** about 62 million.
  - Population groups: Black African 81.4%, Coloured 8.2%, White 7.3%, Indian/Asian 2.7%. These are official statistical categories inherited from apartheid; use them as Stats SA does, without essentialising them.
  - Religion: about 85% Christian.
- **Official languages: 12.** South African Sign Language was added in May 2023 as the 12th.
  - Most spoken at home: isiZulu (24.4%), isiXhosa (16.3%), Afrikaans (10.6%, declining since 1996), Sepedi (about 10%), English (8.7%).
  - English dominates business, government and media.
  - Most South Africans are multilingual. Nguni languages are concentrated in the south-east, and Sotho-Tswana languages inland and in the north.
- Shona is the largest non-official home language, reflecting migration from Zimbabwe.

## History: the standard references

- Leonard Thompson, *A History of South Africa*, and *The Oxford History of South Africa* (2 vols, with Monica Wilson).
- Bill Freund, *Twentieth-Century South Africa: A Developmental History* (Cambridge, 2018).
- Clark & Worger, *South Africa: The Rise and Fall of Apartheid* (2nd ed.).
- Key dates:
  - 1910: Union of South Africa.
  - 1913: Natives Land Act.
  - 1948: National Party wins and apartheid begins.
  - 1960: Sharpeville.
  - 1976: Soweto uprising.
  - 1990: Mandela released.
  - 1994: first democratic election.
  - 1996: Constitution adopted.
  - 1996–98: Truth and Reconciliation Commission.

## Writing and analysis rules

- **Date every statistic.** "33.6% (Stats SA, Q2 2026)", not "one in three".
- **Prefer primary sources:** Stats SA, SAPS, SARB, National Treasury, IEC, HSRC, World Bank, and peer-reviewed or working papers (SALDRU, UNU-WIDER, Growth Lab). Use news (Daily Maverick, News24, GroundUp, Mail & Guardian) for events, not for statistics.
- **Be politically even-handed.** Present party positions, such as on the NHI, expropriation or BEE, as positions rather than verdicts.
- **Avoid the two common distortions:** presenting South Africa as a failed state, or as a resolved success story. The evidence supports neither.
- **Use local terms correctly,** with a short explanation for outsiders: load-shedding, matric, township, informal settlement, spaza shop, BEE/B-BBEE, SOE, "the GNU".
- **Use the rand (ZAR)** for local figures. Add USD only if the reader needs it, and give the date of the exchange rate.
